# SOP: Replacing the hydraulic pump

## Scope
This procedure covers replacement of the Hydraulic pump on model1, model2, model3, and model4. It is intended for trained maintenance personnel authorized to work on hydraulic equipment. Replace the Hydraulic pump when inspection or troubleshooting identifies it as faulty, or as part of scheduled maintenance at the recommended interval of 150 days. Confirm the replacement pump is approved for the specific machine model and matches the required connections and ratings before starting. If the machine’s manufacturer instructions specify a different isolation, removal, installation, or testing method, follow those instructions.

## Safety
Hydraulic systems can retain pressure after shutdown. Pressurized fluid can penetrate skin, and hot fluid or unexpected machine movement can cause serious injury. Never search for a leak with your hand; use a suitable inspection method and obtain medical care immediately for suspected fluid injection.

Before any work on the Hydraulic pump:
- Stop the machine using its normal shutdown procedure, isolate all applicable electrical and other energy sources, and apply lockout/tagout in accordance with site rules.
- Secure or lower any raised loads or machine components. Block or restrain equipment that could move under gravity.
- Relieve hydraulic pressure using the machine’s designated procedure. Verify pressure is released at the specified test points before disconnecting lines; do not loosen a fitting to check for pressure.
- Allow hot components and hydraulic fluid to cool. Wear safety glasses or a face shield, chemical-resistant gloves appropriate for the fluid, protective clothing, and safety footwear.
- Prepare a suitable drain pan and spill-control materials. Avoid skin contact with hydraulic fluid, contain spills promptly, and dispose of used fluid and contaminated materials under site environmental procedures.

Keep the work area clean and prevent dirt from entering open hydraulic ports. Use lifting assistance if the Hydraulic pump is too heavy to handle safely.

## Tools and parts
Prepare the approved replacement Hydraulic pump, any specified replacement seals or gaskets, and the correct hydraulic fluid if topping up is required. Gather the machine’s service documentation, lockout/tagout equipment, hand tools that fit the installed fasteners, approved pressure-relief or test equipment, a drain pan, clean port caps or plugs, lint-free wipes, and spill-control materials. Use a calibrated torque tool if the manufacturer specifies tightening values. Do not substitute seals, fittings, or fluid without confirming compatibility.

## Replacement procedure
1. Confirm the machine model, identify the Hydraulic pump, and verify that the replacement part and required seals are correct.
2. Shut down the machine, isolate all energy sources, apply lockout/tagout, and verify the machine cannot start.
3. Secure movable machine parts and relieve hydraulic pressure using the designated procedure. Confirm pressure is released at the specified test points.
4. Clean the outside of the Hydraulic pump and nearby connections to reduce contamination during removal. Position a drain pan beneath the work area.
5. Label or document the Hydraulic pump’s connections and routing. Disconnect the lines using suitable tools, catch fluid, and immediately cap or plug open lines and ports.
6. Disconnect the pump’s drive or mounting interfaces as specified for the machine. Support the Hydraulic pump before removing its fasteners.
7. Remove the old Hydraulic pump carefully. Inspect the mounting surface, drive interface, and accessible connections for damage or contamination; correct any issue before installation.
8. Compare the replacement Hydraulic pump with the removed unit. Install specified new seals or gaskets, then position the pump without forcing or misaligning the drive.
9. Secure the Hydraulic pump and reconnect the lines and drive components in their documented positions. Tighten fasteners and fittings to the manufacturer’s specified values; do not overtighten.
10. Remove caps and plugs, confirm connections are clean, and replenish or replace fluid as required by the machine instructions. Keep personnel clear while preparing for restart.

## Verification and restart
Check that the Hydraulic pump is secure, all connections are correctly routed, and tools and loose materials are removed. Confirm guards are restored and the work area is free of spills. Follow the machine’s specified priming and restart procedure; do not run the Hydraulic pump dry. Remove lockout/tagout only under site rules, then start the machine from a safe position. Check for abnormal noise, vibration, leaks, and machine response. Stop and isolate the machine if a leak or abnormal condition appears; never tighten a pressurized connection. Record the replacement, fluid added, observations, and any follow-up work in the maintenance log.

## Time estimate
Allow 3 hours for the replacement. Actual time may vary with access, contamination, fluid handling, or additional repairs identified during inspection.
